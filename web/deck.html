A fact carries its own proof. Identity binds it to a hash; a witness checks it independently; the projection travels — so anyone re-derives your knowledge from bytes, with no trust in you.

the problem

Memory that drifts, caches that re-compute, facts you have to trust.

Embedding memory says two equal facts are "similar." A cache keyed on identity re-fetches what it already implies. And a shared corpus inherits whatever the loudest author vouched for. Three leaks — one root cause: the fact isn't bound to its own proof.

the rule

Two questions decide everything.

Does its identity bind the bytes?
Hash the canonical form, and equal facts are one fact. Drift is a type error, not a silent duplicate.
Can a stranger re-derive it?
Ship the generator + witness, and correctness is checked, not trusted. Reputation drops out.
